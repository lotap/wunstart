# Design copy

_Load when writing or changing user-facing copy._

## Content

Plan for empty states when applicable. A search with no results deserves as much consideration as the results.

### Hierarchy

Order by reader need. Lead with the strongest solution or most critical information. Do not invent a call-to-action unless warranted.

### Copy

Do not treat words as decoration. Text should be scannable and convey a message.

Let the surface set the voice, and do not manufacture personality — no fake whimsy or generated-copy slop. Marketing earns belief: for each claim-bearing section, one verifiable claim + proof; one honest next step. App helps finish a task: quiet, short, no selling. If facts are missing, use obviously-placeholder content (lorem ipsum, skeleton), never invented specifics (`process.md` facts rule).

Tone should reflect the expected state the user will be in. Respect their time and energy.

Do not apologize for errors, but explain them and say what the user can do to resolve them.

Avoid decorative labels. If labels are necessary, use specific direct language, not vague titles.

Do not restate or present information the reader already knows.

If removing copy improves the page, keep deleting.

Do not rely on obvious or go-to wording and phrases. They make the whole page feel templated and stale.

#### Use

- Plain speech
- Active voice
- Direct addressing: `you`, not `the user`
- Descriptive subheadings
- Paragraphs of 2-4 sentences
- Sentences around a 20-word target

#### Avoid

- No em/en-dashes or colons as decoration; colons allowed only for labels and price/time headings
- Vague, generic, or filler phrases (e.g. `Unlock your potential`, `in today's fast-paced world`)
- Passive voice
- Rhetorical questions
- Weasel words and weak adverbs (`very`, `really`, `simply`, `just`)
- Hype openers starting with -ing words (`Unlocking…`, `Empowering…`)
- Dense, complicated, or over-compressed sentences that aren't clear on first pass
- Paragraphs longer than 4 sentences
- Subheadings of single generic words
- Prompt language or design commentary
- Use bold only for critical information, never for arbitrary emphasis

Mechanics (sentence case, curly apostrophes, `…`, acronyms, no decorative emoji) live in `contract.md` / `AGENTS.md` — this file is judgment, those lines are law.

Form-level errors and toast patterns (when this file is loaded alone): `references/app.md`.
